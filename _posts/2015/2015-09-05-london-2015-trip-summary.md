This London trip took us from old manuscripts to old computers, with a lot of bookshops, museums, and food in between. My favorite detours were the [Polish Institute and Sikorski Museum](https://pism.org.uk/visits/), [Bletchley Park](https://bletchleypark.org.uk/), and, obviously, the wolf sanctuary. Seeing a pack of wolves up close is hard to beat.

## Where we stayed

London was our base, with day trips to Bletchley and Oxford.

## Places I want to remember

The [British Library](https://www.bl.uk/) had the Magna Carta exhibit, Shakespeare's First Folio, and a Gutenberg Bible. [Temple Church](https://www.templechurch.com/), the [Charles Dickens Museum](https://dickensmuseum.com/), [Benjamin Franklin House](https://benjaminfranklinhouse.org/), [Tate Modern](https://www.tate.org.uk/visit/tate-modern), the [National Gallery](https://www.nationalgallery.org.uk/), [Shakespeare's Globe](https://www.shakespearesglobe.com/), and [Tower Bridge](https://www.towerbridge.org.uk/) filled the London days. I also loved browsing [Persephone Books](https://persephonebooks.co.uk/) and walking [Portobello Road](https://visitportobello.com/).

Outside London, [The National Museum of Computing](https://www.tnmoc.org/) was a fine companion to Bletchley Park. In Oxford we visited the [Bodleian Library](https://www.bodleian.ox.ac.uk/), the [History of Science Museum](https://hsm.ox.ac.uk/), and [Wadham College](https://www.wadham.ox.ac.uk/). Back in London, [Highgate Cemetery](https://highgatecemetery.org/) offered Marx, Douglas Adams, George Eliot, and one very good Lenin joke. Our last outing was for the wolves.
